# Mish Senior — Full-Stack AI-Powered IT Graduate Assessment & Recruitment Platform

## 1. Project Overview

Build a functional, full-stack web application called *Mish Senior* that helps IT graduates demonstrate their skills and helps companies discover qualified candidates through structured assessments and voice-based interviews.

The platform connects two types of users:

1. *Students / Candidates:* Graduates who complete assessments, practice interviews, and official voice interviews to build a candidate profile.
2. *Companies / Recruiters:* Employers who search, filter, and evaluate candidates using their specializations, assessment scores, and interview summaries.

The goal is to build a working recruitment platform, not merely a static UI prototype.

### Non-Negotiable Constraints

* Hackathon duration: 24 hours.
* Maximum Replit credit budget: $100.
* Do not purchase external APIs, subscriptions, or third-party services.
* Do not require paid OpenAI, Whisper, ElevenLabs, or other paid AI or speech services.
* Use Replit's available built-in services and free browser capabilities wherever possible.
* Do not integrate real payment processing.
* Preserve all requirements in this prompt. Do not silently remove features or replace required functionality with a simpler alternative.
* Prioritize a working end-to-end application and a polished hackathon demo.
* Clearly distinguish real functionality from simulated functionality.

If a feature depends on unavailable credentials, browser support, or an external AI service, implement the best honest fallback possible without making the entire application dependent on it.

## 5. Student Journey

Implement the following complete student workflow:

1. Sign in.
2. Choose up to two specializations.
3. Complete a separate text-based assessment for each selected specialization.
4. Determine the official specialization from assessment results.
5. Enforce the pass/fail rule.
6. Unlock practice interviews after passing.
7. Complete voice-based practice interviews.
8. Complete the official voice interview, including HR and technical stages.
9. Save results and available transcripts.
10. Generate an interview performance summary when genuine analysis is available.
11. Display the completed candidate profile.

Persist progress in the database so refreshing the page does not erase completed assessments or interview records.

## 7. Placement Assessments — TEXT ONLY

*Critical distinction: All assessments and tests are text-based. Voice interaction is not required for tests.*

Create timed, role-specific multiple-choice assessments.

### Assessment Requirements

* Each assessment contains 10 questions.
* Each question has multiple answer options.
* Students select answers using the interface.
* Display the question number, progress, answer options, and remaining time.
* Save answers and assessment results in PostgreSQL.
* Calculate the score out of 10.
* Prevent invalid submissions and duplicate score manipulation.
* Calculate the final result on the server.
* Do not require a microphone for any assessment.

### Specialization-Specific Questions

Questions must match the selected specialization.

Examples:

* Flutter Developer: Dart, Flutter widgets, state management, asynchronous programming, and mobile development fundamentals.
* UI/UX Designer: usability, user research, wireframes, visual hierarchy, accessibility, and design principles.
* Frontend Developer: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React fundamentals, and browser behavior.
* Backend Developer: APIs, databases, authentication, server-side concepts, and data validation.

Use a consistent, predefined question bank. If a free AI service is genuinely available, it may help generate additional questions, but the core assessment must not depend on a paid service.

### Assessment Results and Progression

The pass mark is *5/10*.

* Score of 5/10 or higher: the student passes and becomes eligible to unlock the interview stages.
* Score below 5/10: the student fails the assessment and cannot proceed to the official interview stages.

If the student selects two specializations:

* Store both assessment results.
* Compare their scores.
* The specialization with the higher score becomes the official specialization.
* Use that official specialization for the candidate's primary technical interview and recruitment profile.

Define and implement a deterministic tie-breaking rule, such as allowing the student to choose between tied specializations.

### Failed Assessment Experience

When a student fails:

* Show the score and pass threshold.
* Display a supportive explanation.
* Provide relevant learning recommendations resembling a Udemy-style learning recommendation section.
* Recommendations can be curated learning topics and links; do not claim that paid courses have been purchased or unlocked.
* Clearly identify the areas the student should review.

Enforce the pass/fail restriction on the backend. Hiding a button in the frontend is not sufficient.

## 8. Practice Interviews — Voice-Based

Practice interviews must provide a genuine voice interaction experience. They must not be implemented as text-only questionnaires.

### Free Sessions

Each student receives *two free practice interview sessions*.

Track the number of completed or consumed sessions per student in the database.

### Additional Session

After the two free sessions are used:

* Offer an additional practice interview for a simulated price of 1 JOD.
* Show a mock payment interface.
* When the student confirms, record a simulated payment transaction.
* Display a message such as Payment Successful.
* Unlock exactly one additional practice session.

This is a demo payment flow only. Do not connect to a real payment gateway or collect real payment details.

Prevent repeated submissions from unlocking multiple sessions for one simulated transaction.

### Voice Interview Interaction

Each practice interview should:

* Display the interview question.
* Read the question aloud using browser text-to-speech when supported.
* Allow the student to answer using a microphone.
* Use browser speech recognition when supported to capture the spoken response as text.
* Display clear listening, recording, and processing states.
* Provide controls to start or stop listening and to skip a question.
* Show a timer and progress through the session.
* Save questions, available transcripts, timestamps, and session results.

Practice interviews may use a simpler timing policy than the official interview, but they must still function as voice-based sessions.

If the browser cannot support speech recognition, explain the limitation and provide an explicitly labeled fallback. Do not label a typed response as a spoken answer.

## 9. Official Interviews — Voice Is Mandatory

*This is a core feature. The official interview MUST be voice-based. Do not replace it with a text chat, written questionnaire, or typed-answer form.*

The official interview has two stages:

1. HR interview.
2. Technical interview based on the student's official specialization.

### A. HR Interview

The HR stage evaluates the student's ability to introduce themselves, communicate, explain their motivation, and respond to general professional questions.

Requirements:

* Use an interview room designed for spoken interaction.
* Present each question on screen and read it aloud using text-to-speech when supported.
* Require the student to answer through their microphone.
* Do not provide a text field as the primary answer mechanism.
* Enforce a strict total HR interview limit of *10 minutes*.

### B. Technical Interview

The technical stage uses questions related to the student's official specialization.

Examples:

* Flutter Developer: state management, asynchronous programming, widget lifecycle, architecture, APIs, and practical development decisions.
* UI/UX Designer: user-centered design, usability decisions, research, design rationale, and accessibility.
* Frontend Developer: component behavior, browser fundamentals, UI implementation, and debugging.
* Backend Developer: APIs, data modeling, validation, authentication, and system behavior.

Questions should be relevant and varied. Use a predefined question bank if a free AI service is unavailable.

### C. Strict Question Timing

Implement the following behavior for each official interview question:

1. Present the question and read it aloud where supported.
2. Start a *15-second response initiation window* after the question is presented.
3. The student must begin speaking within those 15 seconds or choose to skip.
4. If the student skips, display: Okay, moving to the next question.
5. When the student begins speaking, start the answer timer.
6. The student has a maximum of *60 seconds* for the spoken answer.
7. When 60 seconds is reached, stop capturing the answer and advance to the next question.
8. Allow the student to request a repetition of the current question once per question.
9. Do not allow unlimited question repetitions.
10. Show clear timer, microphone, listening, and question progress indicators.

The 15-second initiation limit and 60-second answer limit must be enforced through application logic. The UI timer alone is not enough.

### D. HR Total Time Limit

The HR interview must have a strict total duration of 10 minutes.

* Track the start time and deadline on the server.
* Enforce the deadline on the backend.
* Stop accepting new answers after the deadline.
* Do not reset the total HR time when the student refreshes the page.
* Ensure that the frontend accurately reflects the remaining time.
* Handle expired sessions consistently.

Do not reset deadlines by navigating away from the interview or reloading the browser.

### E. Browser Voice Support

Use free browser capabilities first:

* Speech synthesis to read questions aloud.
* Speech recognition to capture spoken answers as text.
* Microphone access using browser permission APIs.

Before implementation, test actual browser support in the target environment.

Handle these cases clearly:

* Microphone permission denied.
* Speech recognition unsupported.
* Speech synthesis unsupported.
* No speech detected.
* Browser recognition errors.
* Network interruptions where relevant.
* User refreshes or leaves the interview.
* Interview timer expires.

If a required capability is unavailable, display a clear message and an honest fallback. Do not pretend voice capture or speech recognition succeeded when it did not.

### F. Voice Transcripts and Data Storage

For each official interview, store the available data:

* Interview type and stage.
* Specialization.
* Questions presented.
* Question repetition count.
* Skip events.
* Start and end timestamps.
* Answer duration.
* Recognized speech transcript, when available.
* Completion status.
* Results and feedback.

A browser transcript is not the same as an audio recording. Do not claim to store audio files unless actual audio recording and storage have been implemented.

### G. Interview Performance Summary

After the interview, display a candidate-friendly performance summary.

The summary may include:

* Communication clarity, when it can reasonably be assessed.
* Relevance of answers.
* Strengths.
* Areas for improvement.
* General recommendations.

If a genuine free AI inference service is available, it may analyze the available transcripts and generate the summary.

If no suitable AI service is available:

* Generate transparent, rule-based feedback from available information.
* Label it as basic automated feedback, not AI analysis.
* Do not invent information about the student's answers.
* If there is insufficient transcript data, explain that a reliable content evaluation could not be generated.

The interview itself must remain voice-based even when AI analysis is unavailable.
